Mission

Make institutional authorization post-quantum secure before quantum risk forces an emergency migration.

Protect what exists, now

Every action that can move or change an asset (custody release, issuer roles, collateral release, compliance claims, upgrade and recovery keys) should require a post-quantum signature. EternaX adds that control under the systems institutions already run, keeping their custodian, platform, chain and agreements.

PQ-native settlement, next

For new issuance that wants the chain itself inside the boundary, a second phase brings an EVM network that is post-quantum from genesis. It is optional and not a prerequisite for protecting existing assets; the Pluto testnet is live today.

EternaX defines protection control by control. Nothing is called post-quantum safe while an ECDSA or EdDSA fallback can bypass the post-quantum verifier, and chain consensus, bridges, price oracles and immutable contracts are recorded as documented residuals outside the boundary.

What is EternaX?

EternaX is the post-quantum authorization layer for institutional digital assets. It is not a custodian, tokenization platform or transfer agent, and it never holds keys or funds. Its controls sit under existing custody, tokenization and settlement workflows on Ethereum, EVM chains and Hyperledger Besu.

EternaX Vault is a post-quantum secure smart-contract wallet the institution owns. ETH, stablecoins and any ERC-20 leave it only with NIST post-quantum signatures from the institution's own HSMs, MPC or custodians, for its own holdings or one vault per client. For issuers, EternaX Vault for Issuers, PQ-ONCHAINID, PQ-Permit and PQ-4626 extend the same control to issuer authority, investor compliance, delegated approvals and tokenized-vault governance. For collateral, repo and settlement, PQ-Safe Besu brings post-quantum verifiers to permissioned EVM networks.

EternaX uses hash-based SLH-DSA (FIPS 205) by default, where authority is long-lived, and ML-DSA (FIPS 204) where throughput matters. Policy and threshold authorization are separated from any single signature scheme, so institutions can rotate algorithms later without re-issuing assets or re-onboarding participants.

The work is grounded in published cryptography, open-source reference implementations and institutional exposure research. Institutions start with a 30-day pilot that maps every authority in their path, where it sits, and its status after integration, with no production keys required.
