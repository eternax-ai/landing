01

What is collateral mobility?

Collateral is the asset a market participant puts behind an obligation. In a repo, a cash borrower provides securities (usually high-quality assets such as government bonds) to secure short-term funding. In derivatives and clearing, firms post margin so that a counterparty or central counterparty has protection if exposures move or a participant defaults.

Definition

Collateral mobility is the ability to identify, move, pledge, substitute, release and reuse eligible collateral across counterparties, markets, jurisdictions and systems in time to meet funding and margin obligations.

The simplest way to understand the problem is this: owning collateral is not the same as being able to use collateral. A bank may own billions of dollars of Treasuries, but those securities can still be operationally stranded in the wrong account, legal entity, custodian, market, time zone or settlement window when a margin call or financing need arrives.

The collateral problem is often not “Do we own enough assets?” It is “Can we deploy the right asset, to the right obligation, in the right place, at the right time?”

Supervisors frame the same test. The Bank of England's October 2026 consultation says a CCP must be able to access, transfer and realize collateral reliably when required, including after a clearing-member default. That distinction matters because the economic value comes from usable collateral, not merely from the nominal value of assets on a balance sheet.

02

Why does collateral get trapped?

Traditional markets already have enormous pools of high-quality collateral. The inefficiency comes from fragmentation. A single global institution can manage collateral across hundreds of accounts, multiple custodians, clearing houses, currencies, legal entities and time zones. Each location has its own eligibility rules, cut-offs, reconciliation processes and settlement dependencies.

These frictions create defensive behavior. Firms pre-fund. They over-post. They leave assets in multiple places “just in case.” They hold cash overnight because the security that would otherwise satisfy the obligation may not arrive in time. The result is an operational tax on the balance sheet: more inventory, liquidity and capital are tied up than the economic exposure alone would require.

The prize is therefore not simply faster settlement. It is the ability to make the same stock of high-quality assets do more useful economic work over time.

03

How does tokenization improve collateral mobility?

Tokenization is not the same thing as collateral mobility. Tokenization creates a programmable digital representation or record of an asset or entitlement. Mobility is the ability to use that representation across real financing, margin and settlement workflows. A token that can only sit inside one closed system may be tokenized but still economically immobile.

Capital efficiency
Firms need less idle collateral, less pre-funding and potentially smaller liquidity buffers.

Operationally, tokenization makes availability machine-readable, turns eligibility and haircut rules into code, compresses transfer and pledge into near real time, synchronizes cash and collateral legs through delivery-versus-payment, and lets released collateral be reused sooner, including outside local settlement windows.

Tokenization does not require every asset to leave traditional custody. The MAS Guardian Tokenised Collateral Framework distinguishes three common models: digital twins linked to an existing depository, custodian or register record; custodian-sponsored tokens; and digitally native instruments whose ledger forms part of the official record. The legal and custody model matters as much as the token format.

Tokenization does not automatically create market liquidity. It can improve operational mobility, settlement, programmability and availability. It does not manufacture buyers, sellers or market depth. An economically illiquid asset can remain illiquid even if it becomes easier to transfer.

That is why the market now judges tokenization by what the token can do: a tokenized Treasury financed in triparty repo within minutes, a digital bond accepted as CCP margin, and trillions of dollars of tokenized repo a month. The focus has moved from the token to the financing and margin work it performs.

The category in practice: how JPMorgan built tokenized collateral

No single institution owns tokenized collateral, but one public example spans more of its layers than any other. JPMorgan's Kinexys unit runs collateral, repo, cash and fund products on a permissioned platform and, increasingly, on public blockchains. Its path is the clearest available map of where the market is heading, and of where the cryptographic dependencies follow.

Seven milestones in three years

From one collateral trade to a multi-chain collateral, cash and fund stack

  • Oct 2023Tokenized Collateral Network goes live. BlackRock tokenizes shares in one of its money-market funds and transfers them to Barclays as collateral for an OTC derivatives trade.
  • May 2025First cross-chain DvP touching a public chain. Ondo's tokenized Treasury fund settles on Ondo Chain's testnet against payment on Kinexys, coordinated by Chainlink's runtime environment.
  • Aug 2025Cross-ledger intraday repo with HQLAX and Ownera. Collateral moves on HQLAX, cash settles on Kinexys, with settlement and maturity times set to the minute and up to $1B traded in a day.
  • Nov 2025JPM Coin (JPMD), a USD deposit token, goes live for institutional clients on Base.
  • Dec 2025MONY, J.P. Morgan Asset Management's first tokenized money-market fund, launches on public Ethereum.
  • May 2026JLTXX, a U.S. registered government money-market fund designed to support stablecoin issuers under the GENIUS Act, launches on Ethereum.
  • Oct 2026JPMD goes live on Ethereum; MONY and JLTXX become available on Solana. Kinexys reports $7B in average daily transaction volume and more than $4T processed since inception.

Collateral without liquidation

The Tokenized Collateral Network lets clients transfer collateral ownership while remaining invested. In 2026 it won Best Live Collateral Management Solution at the Future of Finance Awards, alongside Best Traditional Bank.

Precision timing

Intraday repo with settlement and maturity specified to the minute turns idle intraday liquidity into a priced, usable resource.

Multi-chain reach

Deposit tokens and funds now run on Base, Ethereum and Solana, alongside the permissioned Kinexys platform, so clients can choose where collateral and cash live.

Quantum preparation

In May 2024 JPMorgan connected two data centers with a quantum-secured crypto-agile network (Q-CAN). Its CIO described a dual remediation strategy combining post-quantum cryptography and quantum key distribution.

The structural point. Even a bank that has invested visibly in quantum security inherits the signature scheme of every public chain its tokens touch. On Ethereum and Base, accounts authorize transactions with ECDSA over secp256k1; on Solana, with Ed25519. That is a property of the chain, not a choice any issuer makes, and it applies equally to every issuer, custodian and collateral taker on the same rails. No institution, however well prepared, can migrate a shared rail on its own. This report does not assess Kinexys's private infrastructure, whose cryptography is not publicly documented.

When mobility goes multi-chain: the tokenized fund as collateral

The fastest-moving collateral use case on public chains is the tokenized money-market fund. A fund share that keeps earning yield while it secures a trade is exactly the collateral the market has wanted, and venues, regulators and standard-setters moved together in 2026 to make it usable.

Accepted at trading venues

BlackRock's BUIDL, tokenized by Securitize, is accepted as collateral on Crypto.com and Deribit (June 2025), as off-exchange collateral on Binance (November 2025) and on OKX with a regulated bank custodian (April 2026). Franklin Templeton's Benji-issued fund shares back an off-exchange collateral program on Binance (February 2026).

Accepted as derivatives margin

CFTC staff confirmed on 24 September 2026 that swap dealers may accept tokenized money-market fund shares as initial or variation margin for uncleared swaps, subject to conditions. The Bank of England and MAS both examine tokenized money-market funds as collateral in their 2026 papers.

Real scale

Tokenized U.S. Treasuries passed $10B in market capitalization on 11 February 2026 and reached about $13B by 31 March, roughly tripling in fifteen months (CoinGecko 2026 RWA Report).

Multi-chain by design

Securitize uses Wormhole as its official interoperability provider for tokenized funds from BlackRock, Apollo, Hamilton Lane and VanEck, so a fund share can move between chains as its holder needs.

Follow one pledge of a tokenized fund share from holder to collateral taker and count who holds signing keys along the way. Six independently governed cryptographic domains appear, each with its own migration owner. None of them is the fund manager's private infrastructure: they are shared custody, contract, chain, bridge and venue layers.

08

Interoperability as a cryptographic dependency multiplier

Interoperability converts post-quantum migration from a system problem into a coordination problem across independently governed trust domains.

The security of any cross-chain transaction approaches the security of its weakest cryptographic dependency:

Send-to-end ≈ min(S1, S2, S3, … Sn)

where each Si represents the cryptographic security posture of an independently governed trust domain. This property has four consequences.

Weakest-link collapse

If the collateral chain is PQ-safe but a quorum of cross-chain verifier keys remains vulnerable, a future quantum compromise of that quorum could enable forged cross-chain attestations. If the messaging layer migrates but a valuation network remains classical, forged valuation attestations remain a separate route into the workflow. End-to-end assurance therefore depends on every security-critical domain, not only the settlement chain.

Illustrative scenario: if each of 6 participating domains independently achieves 90% probability of completing PQ migration by 2032, the probability that the entire workflow is quantum-safe is 0.96 = 53%. The system retains a 47% probability of having at least one classical dependency. This is a property of the architecture, not of any individual domain's migration effort.

Figure · Illustrative
Weakest-link math: end-to-end PQ assurance falls with every domain
Probability the whole workflow is post-quantum safe by 2032 if each domain independently has a 90% chance of migrating in time.
0.9n. At six domains the workflow is closer to a coin flip (53%) than to the 90% any single operator can promise. Independence is a simplifying assumption; correlated delays would make the curve worse, not better.

Encrypted data exposure

Cross-chain collateral workflows transmit sensitive encrypted data across domains: counterparty positions, collateral valuations, margin calculations, and settlement instructions flow through privacy layers and orchestration services. This encrypted data is subject to harvest-now-decrypt-later risk. An adversary captures ciphertext today and decrypts it when quantum capability arrives, exposing institutional position data and collateral relationships.

Bridge and verifier quorums as systemic dependencies

Cross-chain transfers are authorized by signer quorums that sit outside both the issuer and the collateral taker. Wormhole, for example, documents a canonical set of 19 Guardians and requires 13 of their signatures to produce a valid cross-chain message; those signatures are ECDSA. Its documentation describes future threshold signatures and zero-knowledge proofs, but we found no published post-quantum signature migration. A quantum attacker would need to forge a quorum, not a single node, yet the dependency still sits outside the institution's direct control. The Bank of England's consultation makes the same point in operational terms, asking CCPs whether the failure of a third-party bridge could affect access to collateral.

Migration coordination cost

Cross-domain PQ migration adds a coordination dimension that single-domain migration does not have. Every participating chain, every bridge signer set, every orchestration node, every data feed, and every verifier contract on every destination chain must upgrade. If destination-chain verifier contracts are immutable or upgradeable only through ECDSA-governed multisigs, PQ signatures cannot be verified even if operators produce them. Partial migration provides limited improvement due to weakest-link dynamics.

The threat clock moved in Q4 too

The curves under most of the collateral stack are secp256k1 and other 256-bit elliptic curves: secp256k1 is the default transaction-signing curve of Ethereum and of Besu-based permissioned networks, and ECDSA also signs the bridge guardian and verifier quorums that move tokenized assets between chains. Three new resource estimates this year target that curve directly.

Why a slow machine still matters for collateral. A 26-day attack is far too slow to race a transaction. It is fast enough for a validator, committee, oracle or admin key whose public key has been on record for a year. Those long-lived control-plane keys are exactly the ones a cross-domain collateral workflow depends on. Hardware is moving in the same direction: Google showed magic state cultivation on Willow in October, and IonQ says it is on track for a fault-tolerant 10,000-physical-qubit system in 2027 (a company target, not a demonstrated result).

These are resource estimates under stated hardware assumptions, not machines: no such computer exists today. The Global Risk Institute's Quantum Threat Timeline Report (2025 edition, published March 2026, 26 experts) puts the chance of a cryptographically relevant quantum computer within ten years at 28% to 49%, the highest in the series. IonQ's paper also notes that ML-DSA and SLH-DSA are not affected by this class of attack: the fix is standardized and available now.

Counterargument: “the infrastructure is already becoming quantum-ready”

Bridges and messaging: Wormhole's documentation describes future threshold signatures and zero-knowledge proofs. LayerZero has released Akita, a lattice-based post-quantum polynomial commitment scheme for zero-knowledge proving, which is separate from the ECDSA signatures its verifier networks use today. Conclusion: the research is real, but the signatures that authorize cross-chain messages remain classical.

Besu: stock Besu remains Ethereum-compatible and classical at the transaction and consensus layers. Research and application-layer approaches can add PQ authorization without waiting for a full client rewrite, but those are deployments layered on top of Besu rather than native, default PQ protection.

The institutional point is therefore not that nobody is working on PQ. It is that migration maturity differs by domain. Interoperability forces the end-to-end workflow to inherit those different timelines.

Quantifying the dependency graph

The quantitative exposure is best expressed as a dependency graph, not a signature count. Consensus signatures are block-level, oracle reports serve multiple consumers, and bridge attestations may be batched. What does not reduce through batching or amortization is the number of independently governed cryptographic systems that must migrate.

Regulatory convergence

U.S. federal milestones are a policy baseline, not a blanket mandate on private market infrastructure. Executive Order 14412 (June 22, 2026) directs agencies to move federal high-value assets and high-impact systems to PQC for key establishment by December 31, 2030 and for digital signatures by December 31, 2031. It also calls for a FAR rule requiring covered federal contractors to comply with NIST FIPS, including PQC algorithms, by December 31, 2030.

The more directly relevant signal for collateral infrastructure is the U.S. Treasury's Quantum-Readiness Task Force, announced on August 24, 2026. It operates through three workstreams: sector alignment and the PQC transition; third-party and vendor readiness; and digital assets and emerging technology risk. Its stated focus includes identifying critical dependencies and improving cryptographic agility and interoperability. It builds on the G7 Cyber Expert Group's PQC roadmap for the financial sector, co-chaired by the U.S. Treasury and the Bank of England and published on January 13, 2026.

Collateral rulebooks and PQC roadmaps are being written on different clocks. The Bank of England's draft supervisory statement goes furthest on cryptography: it asks CCPs to consider whether the loss, compromise or unavailability of cryptographic keys, credentials or wallets could prevent them from controlling collateral, and whether a bridge failure could leave collateral inaccessible. It does not mention quantum computing. The MAS Guardian framework treats key management as a holding control and does not set post-quantum requirements. In November, Fed Governor Waller asked how operators of new systems will take responsibility for compromised or lost keys. Institutions that accept tokenized collateral under the first set of rules should expect to be asked how it meets the second.

Evaluation framework

Perimeter coverage. Does the PQ solution cover the entire cross-domain transaction, or only one participating domain? A PQ-hardened collateral ledger connected to classical bridge quorums and classical data feeds provides security within its own perimeter. The end-to-end transaction inherits every external classical dependency, regardless of the settlement infrastructure's own PQ posture.

Coupling and coordination. Does migration require synchronized upgrades across every connected domain? The more tightly operations and providers are coupled, the harder the migration, and cross-chain collateral architectures couple both. Systems built without algorithm-agile abstractions face higher migration costs, which is why crypto-agility belongs in the design review, not the incident review.

Genesis versus retrofit. Is PQ a property of the infrastructure from its origin? Building PQ-native from genesis removes the coordination problem within the settlement perimeter, although external dependencies such as data feeds and messages from classical networks must still be addressed explicitly. Retrofitting PQ across a multi-domain collateral architecture requires coordinated upgrades across chains, bridges, custody and verifier contracts.

Why this cannot wait

Migration debt compounds before the quantum event arrives.

Every new tokenized collateral rail increases the number of cryptographic dependencies institutions must later migrate. The risk is not that tokenization fails. The risk is that tokenization succeeds before its authorization, custody, oracle, messaging and settlement dependencies become post-quantum ready.

The first action is not replacement. It is mapping every control surface, hardening the ones you own, and knowing which ones you must demand from external providers.

Tokenized collateral basics
What is tokenized collateral?
Tokenized collateral is a security, fund share or cash claim recorded on a distributed ledger so it can be pledged, substituted and returned as collateral in repo, derivatives margin or central clearing. Eligibility still depends on legal terms, custody, valuation and the collateral taker's rules. The MAS Guardian Tokenised Collateral Framework (November 2026) distinguishes three models: digital twins, custodian-sponsored tokens and digitally native instruments.
What is collateral mobility?
Collateral mobility is the ability to identify, move, pledge, substitute, release and reuse eligible collateral across counterparties, custodians, markets, jurisdictions and settlement systems when funding or margin obligations arise. The economic goal is to make assets an institution already owns usable in the right place at the right time.
Why does collateral get trapped or sit idle?
Collateral gets trapped when it sits in the wrong account, custodian, jurisdiction or settlement window when an obligation arises. Citi's September 2026 report estimates idle balances can reach about $15 billion at a large institution that manages roughly $74 billion of collateral a day across about 65 custody locations.
What is the difference between tokenization and collateral mobility?
Tokenization creates the programmable digital representation; collateral mobility is the economic capability to deploy that asset where and when it is needed. A token can still be trapped inside one network. Interoperability, legal recognition, eligibility and settlement connectivity are what turn tokenization into mobility.
Does tokenization automatically create market liquidity?
No. Tokenization can improve operational mobility, settlement speed, programmability and availability, but it does not create buyers, sellers or market depth. A tokenized asset can be easier to move and still remain economically illiquid.
What is a tokenized money-market fund and why is it used as collateral?
A tokenized money-market fund records fund shares on a blockchain so they can move like tokens while still earning the fund's yield, which makes them attractive collateral. BlackRock's BUIDL is accepted as collateral on Crypto.com, Deribit, Binance and OKX, and CFTC staff confirmed on 24 September 2026 that swap dealers may accept tokenized money-market fund shares as uncleared-swap margin, subject to conditions.
What is a deposit token?
A deposit token is a commercial bank deposit represented on a blockchain so it can move around the clock and settle against tokenized assets. Examples include JPM Coin (JPMD) on Base and Ethereum, Citi Token Services, live in seven markets, and HSBC's Tokenised Deposit Service, live in six markets.
What is a cross-ledger or cross-chain repo?
A cross-ledger repo is a financing trade in which collateral, cash and orchestration sit on different ledgers that must settle together. For example, J.P. Morgan, HQLAX and Ownera run an intraday repo in which collateral moves on HQLAX and cash settles on Kinexys. The benefit is flexibility; the security consequence is that the trade depends on more independently governed cryptographic systems.

Post-quantum risk to tokenized collateral
Is tokenized collateral quantum safe?
Not end to end today. A tokenized collateral pledge typically passes through custody keys, issuer contract keys, one or more blockchains, bridge signer quorums and the collateral taker's wallet, and public documentation shows classical elliptic-curve signatures (ECDSA or EdDSA) at each of those layers by default. Post-quantum signatures are standardized in NIST FIPS 204 and 205 but not yet deployed across these layers.
What are the post-quantum risks of tokenized collateral interoperability?
Each security-critical trust domain brings its own keys, upgrade authority and migration timeline. A collateral ledger can be post-quantum hardened while messaging, custody, governance or cash-settlement dependencies remain classical, so the workflow can still inherit a vulnerable path.
What is the cryptographic dependency multiplier?
The cryptographic dependency multiplier is the growth in independently governed cryptographic systems when one economic transaction spans multiple chains, custodians, bridges and settlement venues. A cross-chain tokenized fund pledge can involve six trust domains, each with up to seven key classes. Interoperability therefore turns post-quantum migration from a single-system upgrade into a multi-party coordination problem.
What is the weakest-link problem in cross-domain settlement?
End-to-end security is bounded by the weakest security-critical dependency. If six independent domains each have a 90% chance of migrating to post-quantum signatures by 2032, the whole workflow has only a 53% chance of being post-quantum safe, because 0.9 to the sixth power is 0.53.
If one blockchain becomes post-quantum safe, is the full collateral workflow safe?
No. A post-quantum-safe blockchain protects the surfaces inside its own perimeter, but the end-to-end workflow can still depend on classical custody keys, issuer keys, bridge signatures, governance keys or another settlement network. End-to-end assurance requires every security-critical dependency to meet the standard.
Are tokenized deposits quantum safe?
Not by default. Deposit tokens run on bank-operated permissioned ledgers, shared interbank ledgers or public chains, and every mint, burn and transfer is authorized by signing keys. Where deposit tokens circulate on Ethereum or Base, accounts use ECDSA over secp256k1, and permissioned ledgers built on EVM clients such as Besu default to the same curve. Banks can add post-quantum authorization to mint, burn and transfer roles without changing the ledger.
Are tokenized Treasuries and money-market funds quantum safe?
Not end to end. Fund shares such as BUIDL, Benji, MONY and JLTXX live on public chains whose accounts sign with ECDSA (Ethereum and EVM chains) or Ed25519 (Solana and Aptos). Issuer and transfer-agent roles that mint, freeze and allowlist shares use the same classical schemes, and cross-chain transfers rely on ECDSA bridge quorums. This is a property of the chains and bridges, not of any fund manager's design.
Are CSD and market-infrastructure tokenization services quantum safe?
Public documentation points to classical signatures by default. Market-infrastructure tokenization services typically run on permissioned EVM ledgers, which default to secp256k1, or on privacy ledgers whose production signing is classical elliptic-curve cryptography. Settlement bridges and the custody keys that release collateral add further classical dependencies. Because these keys sit underneath every participant's collateral, they are systemic keys.
Is tokenized repo quantum safe?
Not end to end. Tokenized repo platforms authorize every pledge, substitution and release with signing keys on permissioned or Corda-type ledgers, and cross-ledger repo adds a cash ledger and an orchestration layer. Each runs classical signatures by default, and as flow reaches trillions of dollars a month, the value that depends on each signer grows.
Is MPC or HSM custody quantum safe?
No. MPC and HSM custody protect keys from theft, but the signatures they produce are threshold ECDSA or EdDSA matching the chain's curve, which a sufficiently capable quantum computer could forge from the public key. Threshold ML-DSA remains research-stage. Custodians can add post-quantum approvals in front of existing MPC or HSM signing today.
Are cross-chain bridges post-quantum safe?
Not today. Bridges authorize messages with signer quorums: Wormhole, for example, documents 19 Guardians and requires 13 ECDSA signatures for a valid message. Wormhole describes future threshold signatures and zero-knowledge proofs, and LayerZero has released a lattice-based commitment scheme for zero-knowledge proving, but no bridge has published a post-quantum migration of the signatures that authorize messages.
Is Ethereum quantum safe for tokenized assets?
No. Ethereum accounts authorize transactions with ECDSA over secp256k1, and an account's public key becomes visible once it has signed a transaction. Google Quantum AI estimated in March 2026 that breaking 256-bit elliptic-curve keys could take fewer than 500,000 superconducting qubits. Ethereum has no scheduled signature migration, so tokenized assets on Ethereum and L2s such as Base inherit the exposure.
Is Solana quantum safe?
No. Solana accounts sign with Ed25519, an elliptic-curve scheme that Shor's algorithm also breaks. Tokenized funds offered on Solana, including multi-chain share classes, inherit that exposure, and no signature migration is scheduled.
Is Hyperledger Besu quantum safe?
Not by default. Besu signs transactions with the secp256k1 curve by default, with secp256r1 configurable for private networks, and QBFT blocks are sealed with validator signatures. Neither is post-quantum. Post-quantum authorization can be added at the application layer without changing Besu's consensus.
Is Canton Network post-quantum safe?
No. Canton's production signing uses classical elliptic-curve cryptography. Digital Asset said in August 2026 that ML-DSA signing is available only behind an experimental flag, with encryption, wallets, MPC, exchanges, SDKs and the migration of existing parties still to be done.
Is R3 Corda quantum safe?
Not by default. Corda uses EdDSA (Ed25519) for node identity and ECDSA secp256r1 for its certificate authority and TLS. It offers SPHINCS-256 experimentally, and its documentation notes that adding new algorithms requires a network-wide upgrade.
What is harvest-now-decrypt-later risk in tokenized collateral markets?
Harvest-now-decrypt-later means an adversary captures encrypted data today and decrypts it once quantum computing is capable enough. In tokenized collateral it applies to data with long confidentiality lifetimes, such as positions, valuations, margin calculations and settlement instructions. It is a separate risk from signature forgery.
How many qubits would it take to break the keys that authorize tokenized collateral?
2026 estimates fell to roughly 20,000 to 30,000 physical qubits. Google Quantum AI put 256-bit elliptic-curve keys below 500,000 superconducting qubits in minutes (March), IonQ at 19,397 trapped-ion qubits and about 26 days per attempt for secp256k1 (September), and Alice & Bob at 29,588 cat qubits and about an hour (October). These are resource estimates, not existing machines, and ML-DSA and SLH-DSA are not affected by this class of attack.
When could quantum computers break today's collateral keys?
No machine can do it today. The Global Risk Institute's Quantum Threat Timeline Report (2025 edition, published March 2026, 26 experts) puts the chance of a cryptographically relevant quantum computer within ten years at 28% to 49%, the highest in the series. Because collateral keys stay in use for years and migration across many trust domains takes years, institutions are planning now.
Regulation & supervisory expectations
Do the new tokenized-collateral rules address post-quantum risk?
Not directly. The Bank of England's October 2026 consultation asks CCPs to consider the loss, compromise or unavailability of cryptographic keys and the failure of third-party bridges, but does not mention quantum computing. The MAS Guardian framework treats key management as a holding control without post-quantum requirements. Post-quantum expectations are set separately, through the G7 Cyber Expert Group roadmap, the U.S. Treasury's Quantum-Readiness Task Force and Executive Order 14412.
What does the Bank of England's tokenised collateral consultation cover?
The Bank of England's 29 October 2026 consultation sets draft supervisory expectations for CCPs accepting tokenised versions of eligible collateral, covering liquidity, valuation and interoperability; settlement finality; credit and concentration risk; operational resilience; and custody and safeguarding. It asks CCPs to consider key loss or compromise and bridge failure. Responses are due 12 February 2027.
What is the MAS Guardian Tokenised Collateral Framework?
It is an industry framework published on 19 November 2026 by a workgroup convened by MAS, setting out eight lifecycle steps for tokenised collateral: agreement, eligibility and setup; delivery and settlement finality; legal effectiveness and priority; holding; life of the trade; return; default and enforcement; and cross-border considerations. It covers repo, uncleared derivatives margin and CCP margin.
Can tokenized collateral be used as derivatives margin in the US?
Yes, within conditions. CFTC staff issued tokenized collateral guidance and announced a pilot program in December 2025, and on 24 September 2026 confirmed that swap dealers may accept tokenized money-market fund shares as initial or variation margin for uncleared swaps, provided the underlying fund meets existing eligibility rules and the tokens carry equivalent rights.
What U.S. post-quantum deadlines apply to financial infrastructure?
Executive Order 14412 (22 June 2026) sets federal targets of 31 December 2030 for post-quantum key establishment and 31 December 2031 for post-quantum digital signatures in high-value federal systems, and calls for covered federal contractors to meet PQC-inclusive FIPS by 31 December 2030. These are migration signals, not a blanket mandate on every private market infrastructure.
What is the U.S. Treasury Quantum-Readiness Task Force?
Announced on 24 August 2026, it is a public-private initiative to coordinate the U.S. financial sector's move to post-quantum cryptography. It works through three workstreams: sector alignment and the PQC transition, third-party and vendor readiness, and digital assets and emerging technology risk. It builds on the G7 Cyber Expert Group roadmap published in January 2026.

What are ML-DSA and SLH-DSA?
ML-DSA and SLH-DSA are NIST's standardized post-quantum digital signature algorithms: ML-DSA (FIPS 204) is lattice-based and SLH-DSA (FIPS 205) is hash-based. Both are final standards and resist the quantum attacks that break ECDSA and EdDSA. EternaX uses SLH-DSA by default and ML-DSA where needed.

Can Bitcoin collateral be protected against quantum attack today?
Yes, at the custody layer. The EternaX Bitcoin Vault holds BTC in a one-time P2WSH output built from 100 fresh keys, so only a SHA-256 hash is on-chain until the coins move, and every spend must first be approved with NIST post-quantum signatures (SLH-DSA or ML-DSA). PQ-native settlement makes post-quantum authorization a property of the settlement perimeter from genesis, reducing the legacy classical dependencies that must be coordinated later.
