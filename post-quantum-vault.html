SLH-DSA (NIST FIPS 205) and ML-DSA (NIST FIPS 204) are post-quantum digital signature standards published by NIST in August 2024. SLH-DSA is hash-based, the most conservative assumption, and is EternaX Vault's default. ML-DSA is lattice-based with smaller signatures, and serves HSMs without SLH-DSA firmware and post-quantum MPC signers.

For EternaX Vault, HSMs with published SLH-DSA and ML-DSA support include Entrust nShield 5, Securosys Primus, Futurex, Marvell LiquidSecurity and Crypto4A. Thales Luna and Utimaco support ML-DSA, with SLH-DSA to confirm per firmware version, and Thales TCT Luna T-Series supports ML-DSA. EternaX validates each integration on your hardware during the pilot.

Yes. MPC platforms that sign with threshold ECDSA or EdDSA, such as Fireblocks, Copper or Dfns, are not post-quantum, but they can remain your approval workflow while the post-quantum key sits in your HSM. Post-quantum MPC signers that produce ML-DSA can sign EternaX Vault approvals directly.

Crypto-agility is the ability to replace cryptographic algorithms without rebuilding the system around them. EternaX Vault adds new signature schemes through a governed registry, lets approvers rotate while mixed quorums keep payments flowing, and retires weakened schemes with a security floor. The vault address, assets, quorum and audit history never change.

EternaX Vault accepts only post-quantum signatures, while multisigs such as Safe use ECDSA owners today and only count signatures. EternaX Vault also adds a second independent approval, so stolen signing keys are not enough, and changes algorithms by key rotation rather than redeployment.

Funds stay safe. The EternaX Vault coordinator holds no keys and cannot create or change an approval. Each approver signs in their own app, which re-checks every payment against its own limits and never signs a blind hash, and the vault verifies every signature on-chain.

In EternaX Vault, approver changes go through your admin quorum under a settings time lock, and the vault refuses any change that would leave too few approvers to reach the quorum, so rotation can never lock funds. Approval shares are refreshed in a new ceremony, and any approver can freeze the vault in an emergency.
