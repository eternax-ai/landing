Bunker mode is the defensive posture Ethereum Foundation researcher Justin Drake asked the blockchain industry to start planning for on 7 October 2026, after warning that ECDSA, the signature scheme behind Bitcoin and Ethereum, could break before quantum computers arrive, in the worst case within months, as AI accelerates mathematics. It means moving funds to fresh addresses whose public key is still hidden behind a hash. That protects idle funds only until the next signature. Institutions sign every day, so they need a bunker that stays closed while they keep transacting: post-quantum approval of every payment. EternaX Vault provides that with NIST post-quantum signatures, by default hash-based SLH-DSA, the kind of cryptography Drake has urged Ethereum to accelerate toward. Crypto-agility, or cryptographic agility, is the ability to replace cryptographic algorithms, parameters and keys without rebuilding the system around them. For digital assets it decides whether the next algorithm change is a routine key rotation or another migration with re-issued assets, re-onboarded participants and re-papered agreements. Assets issued in 2026 will still exist after 2035, when NIST's draft guidance disallows ECDSA and EdDSA, and post-quantum standards will keep evolving. Executive Order 14412 of 22 June 2026 directs US federal agencies to move to NIST post-quantum standards and to help critical-infrastructure operators do the same. Crypto-agility turns that transition into an operating routine rather than a one-time project.

EternaX separates who approves from which algorithm they sign with, so policy and threshold authorization stay the same while schemes change. In EternaX Vault, new signature schemes enter through a governed registry: each module passes a self-test and a 7-day activation window that any single approver can veto. Approvers then rotate while mixed quorums keep payments flowing, and a security floor retires weakened schemes; strength only goes up and classical schemes are refused. The vault address, assets, quorum, policies and audit history never change. EternaX Vault passes 26 of 26 crypto-agility tests, including a payment approved by SLH-DSA and ML-DSA approvers together.

A CBOM, or cryptographic bill of materials, is a machine-readable inventory of the algorithms, keys, certificates, libraries and modules a system depends on, and it is the starting point for post-quantum migration; Executive Order 14412 directs US agencies to publish CBOM guidance within 270 days. EternaX supports both NIST post-quantum signature standards published in August 2024, chosen per boundary. SLH-DSA (FIPS 205) is hash-based, relying only on the security of hash functions, the most conservative assumption in a NIST standard; it is the default for long-lived authority such as issuer, upgrade and recovery keys and for HSMs that support it. ML-DSA (FIPS 204) is lattice-based with smaller signatures (about 2.4 KB versus 7.9 KB), and serves HSMs without SLH-DSA firmware and post-quantum MPC signers. FN-DSA (FIPS 206) is treated as a future activation option. These are NIST-standardized algorithms; FIPS 140-3 validation applies per HSM product.

EternaX uses only NIST-standardized post-quantum signature algorithms, SLH-DSA (FIPS 205) and ML-DSA (FIPS 204), and accepts no ECDSA or other quantum-vulnerable signature on a protected path. Its on-chain SLH-DSA verifier passes all 56 NIST test vectors and rejected 600 of 600 corrupted signatures in fuzzing. FIPS 140-3 validation applies to cryptographic modules, so it sits with the HSMs that generate and hold the keys: institutions use their own validated HSMs, and validation status is confirmed per vendor and firmware during the pilot. EternaX is the post-quantum authorization layer for institutional digital assets, built by EternaX Labs. EternaX lets banks, custodians, asset managers, market infrastructures and stablecoin and tokenized-asset issuers make their digital assets post-quantum secure, or quantum-safe, starting now, without waiting for their chain to upgrade or rebuilding their custody stack. With EternaX, every action that can move or change an asset (custody release, stablecoin and tokenized-asset roles, collateral pledge and release, compliance claims, and upgrade and recovery keys) requires NIST post-quantum signatures: hash-based SLH-DSA (FIPS 205) by default and ML-DSA (FIPS 204) where needed. EternaX is crypto-agile: institutions rotate algorithms without re-issuing assets or re-onboarding participants. EternaX Vault is a post-quantum secure smart-contract wallet that the institution owns, for treasury and client holdings, approved by the HSMs, MPC or custodians it already uses. For stablecoins and tokenization, EternaX provides EternaX Vault for Issuers, PQ-ONCHAINID, PQ-Permit and PQ-4626. For collateral, repo and settlement, EternaX provides PQ-Safe Besu on Hyperledger Besu and other permissioned EVM networks. EternaX runs on Ethereum, EVM chains and unmodified Besu, and records chain consensus as a documented residual outside its boundary. The EternaX on-chain SLH-DSA verifier passes all 56 official NIST test vectors, and EternaX cryptography is published in three arXiv research papers, including SILMARILS. EternaX Vault is live on testnet, institutions start with a 30-day pilot from kickoff, and an optional PQ-native EVM settlement network is available for new issuance. EternaX publishes the Post-Quantum Exposure Map, Quantum Value at Risk (QVaR) and the P(Q Doom) index. EternaX was founded by Dariia Porechna, Paarrthhh Birla and Dr. Chen Feng.
