The authorization path

Every tokenized asset still moves on a classical signature somewhere in the path you own.

Custody release, issuer and treasury roles, compliance claims, upgrade and recovery keys, and the chain itself all accept ECDSA or EdDSA today. NIST's draft guidance deprecates those algorithms after 2030 and disallows them after 2035. Waiting for a chain vendor or a participant's custodian hands that decision to parties you do not govern, and none of them covers your own admission, issuer and recovery authority.

Protect what exists

Three surfaces. One policy. No re-platforming.

The controls sit under the systems you already operate, on your own Besu network or on Ethereum. On Ethereum and other public EVM chains, contract-level authority is PQ-enforced and the chain's own signatures are recorded as a documented residual. Both FIPS signature standards are selectable per boundary: ML-DSA where throughput matters, hash-based SLH-DSA where authority is long-lived. Policy is independent of the scheme, so you rotate algorithms without re-issuing assets or re-onboarding participants, and there is no second migration if one algorithm family weakens.

Custody

PQ Custody SDK

Dual-gate authorization under MPC, HSM and Safe-style custody. Each approver authenticates with a post-quantum signature; a Shamir-shared seal enforces the threshold before the action is released. Both NIST FIPS signature standards, chosen per boundary: ML-DSA (FIPS 204) where throughput and signature size matter, and hash-based SLH-DSA (FIPS 205) where authority is long-lived. Policy and threshold authorization are independent of the scheme, so the institution can rotate algorithms without re-issuing assets or re-onboarding participants. FN-DSA (FIPS 206) is treated as a future activation option, not a production path.

The assets issued in 2026 will still exist in 2035, and the keys that govern them are the hardest to rotate. SLH-DSA reduces to the security of hash functions, the most conservative assumption available in a NIST standard. Anchoring long-lived authority there, with signature-agnostic policy on top, means that if one algorithm family weakens the institution does not face a second migration across its issuer, upgrade and recovery keys.

A post-quantum signer at the custodian protects the custodian's own release path. It does nothing for issuer roles, compliance claims, upgrade keys and recovery paths in the institution's contracts, or for admission authority at its control points. A chain vendor's roadmap sets a timeline and an algorithm the institution does not control. EternaX puts the policy decision at the institution's control points, so its custodians and participants inherit it, and the institution chooses and rotates the algorithm itself.
